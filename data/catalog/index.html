β of each expression

Each dot is one expression; the x-axis is its complexity. β is the decay rate of the exponential law : rank the operator types by how often they occur, and each step down the ranking multiplies the expected frequency by e−β. β is the log-least-squares estimate: each rank contributes one residual, and β minimises , where is the observed frequency at rank . A rare rank pulls as hard as a common one. A dot uses one expression's own ranks; the horizontal line uses every expression of the corpus, counts added together and ranked once. The ± after the corpus β is a bootstrap 1σ: the expressions are resampled with replacement 2,000 times, re-ranked and refitted. Feynman is the released 100. Wikipedia adds the algebraic extension after Doppler. f(R) is included.

β by branch

Each dot is one Feynman expression in that branch. The x-axis is its complexity and the y-axis is its own β. The line is the branch fit after the expressions' counts are added together. Benchmark dots open the benchmark; lecture dots open the Lectures. The random-model card uses one Motzkin tree per expression, of the same complexity, with operator labels drawn uniformly.
